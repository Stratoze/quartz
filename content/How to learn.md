#### Testing effect:
- **it's better to learn something in a testing and feedback cycle**
- a derivative of this is the **spacing effect** which is just a logically spaced out testing effect
- Integral part of the **Master Method**

#### Spaced Repetition:
- Basically just use anki
#### Master Method:
- The best way to become a master of something is to piggyback off of master's work
- Basically, after chunking we **look at masters' works and then try to replicate them and then test ourselves on which part we did differently to theirs and think of why, then note it down to force our brain to make connections**
- example: in chess, look at a grandmaster's past chess match, choose a position and then try to make moves for both sides and then compare it to what the grandmasters did, reflect on why they did differently than us and note it down
- example: in art find art we like, take a part of it, say the eyes and then try to replicate it without reference, then use reference to see where they differ from us and try to infer why and then note it down
- example: in cyber security we try to do ctfs ourself and then see how pros do it in walk through and infer why they did certain things, which part of what we did was low leverage(aka trying to find a way to reverse engineer a pwn challenge first, can be done but low leverage)
- based on the testing > feedback > testing cycle

### Things needed to become an expert: 
- **Repeated attempts with feedback:** (Not necessarily 10.000 hours, that's just a research based on the top 0.1% violin players... doesn't apply to all fields)
- **Timely feedback**, preferably instant feedbacks
- **Valid environment** (somewhat predictable, not just luck based)
- **Don't get too comfortable** (there's 3 zone, comfort, uncomfortable and impossible zone, **you want to be in the uncomfortable zone**)

### 85% rule:
- Why we **don't** want to be in the impossible zone
- **it's best to do something you can do around 85% successfully**, it prevents burn out while not being too comfortable, how to know if you're in the 85% zone without quantitative feedback ? basically something challenging that is still doable
	- (extra) in negotiation counter offering with 85% of the offered pricing often works
	- (extra) in training 85% of 1 RM is often sufficient for hypertrophy

### Self determination theory:
 - **Competence:** You need to feel that you're improving
	 - Track the time you spent doing something
	 - Keep a progress archive of sort
	 - **Fail forward**, failures signal what you can improve, hence **preservation is underrated**
 - **Relatedness:** Connecting with others, fictional or real
	 - Communities
	 - **Comparison (admiration)**
- **Autonomy:** Freedom of choice
	- Don't please everyone, the optimal ratio for engagement and success is actually having 50% of people liking you and the other half hating you **(Barbell Strategy)**

#### **Comparison is a good form of feedback/self reflection, just don't let it affect you emotionally, Stoicism works wonder to fix that**

### **Extrinsic motivation is fragile, it's best to be anti fragile**
- **Barbell strategy:** go into the 2 extremes, the middle is fragile
- **Don't try to predict too far ahead:** the world is chaotic
- **Give yourself options:** burn the boat, don't limit yourself to a fixed path (aka ideology or morality)
- **Don't give a fuck about stuff you know doesn't matter just to fit it**

### Eat the frog: 
- **do the hardest most important stuff first**
- if something is hard but not important consider removing it from the list

### 20/80 rule:
- aka the **pareto principle** 
- **most of the time 20% of the process dictates 80% of the outcome**
- **focus on the 20% of fundamentals that matters first** (often ones with high **skills transferability** and **high leverage**) (mostly via books or courses) in the 20% of fields that matters (mostly individualized)

### Chunking: 
- **break problems into smaller parts first**
- easily combined with other techniques like pomodoro

### Pomodoro: 
- **Split a work into a work and rest cycle based on focus and motivation level**, generally from 15-45 minutes for work and 5-15 minutes for rest (which can be used for working out or meditation, since both are proven to improve cognitive function)
- **continuous working duration shouldn't exceed 1 hour at a time**
- the total amount of time working and resting continuously is a **working block**, which **shouldn't exceed 4 hours** at a time
- there should be a big break between working blocks
- why ? **working and rest interval** for preventing burnout and boosting cognitive functions (because our brain transfer knowledge to the long term memory better during rest periods, same principle with muscle hypertrophy) 

### Expertise reversal effect: 
- **don't try to copy things experts too far ahead of you do for improvement**, it is often for them to scrape that diminishing return
- **improve the fundamentals that matters first**
- example: don't try to do a split as a beginner, just do full body, even 1 set per exercise is ok, try to workout like an expert and you will get 2% extra gains for 1000% more difficulty

Example Derived Work Flow

#### Specify the subject path to mastery, does it
- allow for repeated attempts with feedback
- has timely feedback
- has a solid environment
#### Then we break it down to smaller problems
- how does the overall skill tree looks like to our needs ?
- what are the most high leverage/ high transferability skill set from the subject ?
#### And into even smaller problems
- find an existing roadmap or a book for it
- find what we need to learn
- break it down into smaller chunks (category/sub skills)
- create a note on the subject and each sub skills
- allocate daily time for it in a way so that consistency will be guaranteed
#### Beginner stage
- select a chunk to learn, break it into even smaller chunks
- prioritize important but not urgent chunks first (unless extremely urgent)
- then important and urgent chunks
- the rest can be discarded
- fit each chunk into a pomodoro session
- repeat until reaching intermediate stage
#### Intermediate stage (after 20% completion of chunks)
- do the same as beginner stage but now we have enough knowledge and self awareness to analyze
- find relevant high level works
- use master method
- repeat until finding someone better is no longer feasible
#### Advanced stage
- here be dragon
- called advanced but at this point you're at the top of humankind in that field
- can stop here and focus on other fields or do active researches
- beware of diminishing return
