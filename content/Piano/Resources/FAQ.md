# FAQ

## I do not feel like practicing today
Normal. The fix is not willpower; it is reducing friction.
- Anchor it: After coffee, laptop, or brushing teeth, I sit at the piano.
- Start absurdly small: I will just play one scale.
- Use the 2-minute rule: commit to 2 minutes. You may stop after 2.
- Forgive the skip. Missed a day? Fine. Missed three? Still fine.
- One more reframe: you are not "someone trying to practice piano.", You are a pianist. Pianists sit down. Some days they play one scale. That is not a failed session; that is a pianist's Tuesday. The goal is not to feel motivated. The goal is to be the kind of person who sits down. Motivation follows action, not the other way around.

## This piece is impossible or I am not improving
- You probably are improving. Results often appear days or weeks later.
- If stuck 2+ weeks, use [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md#plateau-breaking).
- Check whether the piece is mis-leveled. See [Repertoire and 12-Week Goals](Repertoire%20and%2012-Week%20Goals.md#shelving-pieces).
- Play something you love for 5 minutes. Remember why.

## Practice feels mechanical or joyless
- Give passages a character: thunderstorm, lullaby, question, answer.
- Sing the phrase. Make it breathe.
- Improvise for 5 minutes. No rules, no goals.
- Listen to a recording that moves you.
- Remember: you play music, not just piano.

## What if I keep making the same mistake?
- Do not repeat the same error on autopilot.
- Use error triage: [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md#learning-protocol).
- Isolate the exact spot: [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md#q-spots).
- Change one variable: tempo, chunk size, hands, rhythm, articulation.

## What if I can play it slowly but not at tempo?
- Slow mastery is necessary but not sufficient.
- Use [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md#speed-work) only after notes and fingering are secure.
- Use chaining and rhythm variations in short bursts.
- End in control, not degraded.

## What if I have tension or pain?
- Stop immediately for pain, weakness, tingling, numbness, swelling, loss of dexterity, or increasing tension.
- Reduce speed and dynamic.
- Reconsider fingering, movement, bench height, and distance.
- Do not force stretches or play through pain.
- Persistent pain: see a professional familiar with musicians.

## How much should I practice?
- Consistency beats completeness.
- Minimum session: 15–25 minutes.
- Standard session: 40–60 minutes.
- Long session: split into blocks with real breaks.
- Reduce during busy or fatigued weeks.

## Should I use the metronome all the time?
No. Use it as a check tool.
- Use it after rhythm and movement are understood.
- Overuse flattens expression.
- Turn it off when testing musical shaping.

## Should I record every repetition?
No. Record:
- one weekly performance take
- a baseline when beginning a significant piece
- checkpoints and final performances
- occasional short diagnostic clips
- monthly video for posture and tension

## Are piano apps enough?
No. Apps assess note and rhythm accuracy only.
They do not assess dynamics, articulation, tone, pedaling, or musicality.
Use app scores as one data point, then use recordings, rubric, and external feedback.

## When should I shelve a piece?
Shelve without guilt if:
- blocked 3+ weeks despite plateau-breaking
- you no longer feel motivated by it
- it turns out to be mis-leveled
Shelving is data, not failure.

### Sleep and timing

- Your brain consolidates motor skills between sessions, especially during sleep.
- Daily practice + good sleep beats weekend marathons. This is not motivational advice; it is neuroscience.
- If you can choose, practicing closer to bedtime gives sleep more material to consolidate.
- Protect sleep duration. A 45-minute session followed by 8 hours of sleep outperforms a 2-hour session followed by 5 hours.
- Naps after practice may also help: brief post-training rest periods provide consolidation benefits.
## The meta-rule
A theoretically perfect plan you abandon in three weeks loses to a simpler plan you run for three years.
