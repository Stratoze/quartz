## Practice Volume and Pacing

| Practice pattern | Expected effect |
|---|---|
| 15 to 25 min minimum | keeps habit alive |
| 40 to 60 min standard | steady progress |
| 60 to 90 min serious | faster progress if recovery is good |
| 90 to 120 min advanced | diploma-track if sustainable |
| 2 hours plus | concert-track, requires breaks and health monitoring |

Consistency beats intensity.
A plan you can run for 15 years beats a plan you burn out on in 15 months.

### Deload Weeks

Every 4th or 5th week, or whenever fatigue accumulates, take a deload:

- Cut practice time by 40–50%.
- Drop tempo on everything by 20–30%.
- No new material. Play only things you already know and enjoy.
- Extra sleep, extra listening, extra score study away from the piano.
- No guilt. This is not a missed week. It is a consolidation week.

Signs you need a deload:

- Practice feels like a chore for 4+ days straight.
- You are making more errors than usual on familiar material.
- Mild tension or soreness that does not resolve overnight.
- You dread sitting down.

A deload week is not quitting. You sit down every day. You just play less, slower, and for pleasure.

## Implementation Order

Do not add everything tomorrow. Phase it in.

### Day 1
Sit down, play, 15 minutes.

### Weeks 1 to 2: Repair the core
- Use the adaptive tempo protocol, not a rigid tempo algorithm.
- Use error triage instead of binary pass/fail.
- Define chunks explicitly before starting a piece.
- Choose HS or HT based on the actual problem.
- Begin musical work immediately.
- Stop recording every repetition.
- Use two concurrent pieces: learning and polishing.
- Use outcome targets for method book work, not page counts.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Repair repertoire management
- Establish learning, polishing, and maintenance pool.
- Convert Anki cards from song titles to specific tasks.
- Limit Anki review by time, not card count.
- Add a roughly 2-week safety net.
- Lower maintenance entry bar to performance-ready.

### Month 2: Add musicianship
- Sight-reading, 5 min, 3 to 5 times per week.
- Ear and theory, 2 to 3 times per week.
- One weekly chord, lead-sheet, improv, or by-ear session.
- Start the listening habit.
- Add scales, arpeggios, and technical requirements to the technique rotation.

### Month 3: Add long-term structure
- Identify your current stage from [Roadmap](Roadmap.md).
- Select exact etudes by technical objective.
- Set a 12-week outcome and write it down.
- Arrange one form of external feedback.
- Record a mock recital at the end of the cycle.

## Starting from Zero

For the first sitting only. After that, use the Daily Card.

### Setup
- Bench height: sit tall, drop hands to the keys; forearms roughly parallel to the floor, wrists level with keys.
- Distance: elbows slightly forward of the ribs, not tucked in.
- Score at eye level, well lit.
- Metronome nearby. Pencil in reach.
- Phone in another room, or set up to record.

### The first practice day
1. Sit down. Play a scale slowly, twice. Notice how your body feels.
2. Open your method book or current piece to where you are.
3. Pick one short section, 2 to 4 measures.
4. Play it slowly: hands separately if hard, together if easy.
5. Clean? Move on. Not clean? Use error triage in [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md#learning-protocol).
6. Stop after 15 minutes. Write one journal line.

## Technical progression ladder

### Scales and chords

| Stage | Technical focus |
|---:|---|
| 0 | pentascales, one-octave scales, simple triads |
| 1 | two-octave scales, contrary motion, chromatic one octave |
| 2 | two-octave hands together, simple arpeggios, root-position triads |
| 3 | three-octave scales, dominant seventh chords, diminished introduction |
| 4 | four-octave scales, arpeggio inversions, diminished seventh chords |
| 5 | octaves, broken octaves, chord progressions, advanced voicing |
| 6 | octaves, chords, leaps, repeated notes, stamina |
| 7 | concert technique: orchestral sonority, large chords, advanced pedaling |
| 8 | transcendent virtuosity: Liszt-level leaps, chords, velocity, endurance |

### Etude ladder

| Stage | Etudes |
|---:|---|
| 0 | method book, Beyer, very easy Lemoine |
| 1 | Gurlitt, Köhler, Czerny Op.599 and Op.139 selected, Bertini easy |
| 2 | Burgmüller Op.100 easier, Czerny Op.849 selected, Schytte, Loeschhorn |
| 3 | Burgmüller Op.100 broader, Heller Op.45 selected, Czerny Op.299 selected |
| 4 | Heller Op.46, Czerny Op.299, Cramer selected, Moscheles selected |
| 5 | Czerny Op.740 selected, Moscheles, Cramer, Moszkowski easier, Saint-Saëns easier, Chopin easier etudes |
| 6 | Chopin Op.10 and Op.25 selected, Moszkowski Op.72, Saint-Saëns, Liszt concert etudes selected |
| 7 | Chopin broader, Liszt Transcendental and Paganini selected, Debussy, Scriabin, Rachmaninoff, Alkan selected |
| 8 | Liszt Transcendental major selection, Alkan, Saint-Saëns, Ligeti selected, Kapustin optional |

## Musicianship progression

### Sight-reading
- always roughly two stages below current repertoire
- keep moving
- prioritize pulse, bass, and contour
- do not turn sight-reading into repertoire practice

### Ear and theory
- sing before playing
- identify intervals and chords
- recognize cadences and progressions
- harmonize simple melodies
- play from lead sheets
- improvise short answers

### Listening
- current pieces in multiple recordings
- repertoire one stage above
- score-following when possible
- active listening with one question per session

## Readiness Stages and Shelving

### Learned
Notes, rhythm, and fingering mostly stable in sections. Musical intentions identified.

### Performance-ready
Plays through with musical continuity. Minor blemishes allowed.

Heuristic criteria:

- plays through without major breakdown
- can start from several important structural points
- can play the hardest section without preparatory reps
- maintains pulse and recovers after a minor mistake
- plays with intended dynamics, articulation, voicing, pedaling
- no pain or excessive tension
- passes on at least two separate days

### Secure
For exams or real performances:

- one uninterrupted recorded take
- a successful cold start on another day
- reliable random starts
- musical continuity despite a small slip
- physical and mental control sustained to the end

### Maintained
Recoverable efficiently after increasing intervals.

Perfection is asymptotic. It is not the entry requirement.

## Shelving Pieces

Shelve without guilt if:

- blocked 3+ weeks despite plateau-breaking
- you no longer feel motivated by it
- it turns out to be mis-leveled

Return in three months and it may go quickly. Or never return. Two concurrent pieces must never become two concurrent obligations.

## Method Book Protocol

Work by outcome, not by page.

### A section is complete when
- the new concept is understood and can be applied
- the piece or exercise can be played through with control
- musical intent, not just notes, has been addressed

### What counts as progress
- one new concept understood and applied
- one short piece playable at a comfortable tempo with musical intent
- one identified technical skill executed reliably

### When to stop the method book
- Standard repertoire at your stage is no longer harder than the method book material.
- You complete each new section in one session because nothing is genuinely new.
- Honest self-assessment or a teacher places you at a stage where the method book is review.

Then transition to graded repertoire and etudes. Do not restart a higher method book from page one; move directly into standard repertoire at the right stage.

### Apply the learning protocol
Method book pieces get the same treatment as repertoire: preview, chunking, appropriate HS/HT, error triage, musical intent from session one.

## Macrocycle and Cycle Structure

| Level | Scope | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Macrocycle | Roadmap stage, 1–3 years | What skills and repertoire define this era? |
| Mesocycle | 12-week goal | What specific pieces and technical targets? |
| Microcycle | Weekly review | What did I do, what is stuck, what is next? |
| Session | Daily card | What am I improving today? |

At the end of each 12-week cycle:

1. Record the mock recital.
2. Score it with the rubric.
3. Compare to the previous cycle's recording.
4. Update the macrocycle: am I still in the same Roadmap stage?
5. Set the next 12-week goal using [12-Week Goal](../Templates/12-Week%20Goal.md) template.
6. Take a deload week before starting the new cycle.

## Liszt Path

- This pathway reflects the original author's long-term artistic goal (Liszt). Treat it as one example of an advanced specialization, not the only destination.

### Prerequisites
Before serious Liszt, you should have:

- secure four-octave scales and arpeggios
- octaves, broken octaves, chords, and leaps
- repeated notes, trills, and chromatic velocity
- ability to voice melody over thick texture
- half pedaling and dynamic pedaling
- 30 to 45 minute continuous playing stamina
- reliable memory and structural starts
- performance experience

### Gateway sequence
1. Liszt Consolation No.3 or No.5
2. Liszt Liebestraum No.3
3. Liszt Sonetto 104 del Petrarca
4. Liszt Sonetto 123 del Petrarca
5. Liszt Vallée d'Obermann
6. Liszt Three Concert Etudes selected
7. Liszt Paganini Etudes selected, especially La campanella with teacher support
8. Liszt Transcendental Etudes selected by objective
9. Liszt Mephisto Waltz No.1
10. Liszt Hungarian Rhapsodies selected
11. Liszt Sonata in B minor
12. Liszt Dante Sonata and Années de pèlerinage cycles

### Transcendental Etudes by objective

| Objective | Useful numbers |
|---|---|
| Evenness and sonority | No.1 Prelude, No.3 Paysage, No.6 Vision |
| Velocity and leaps | No.2 Fusees, No.4 Mazeppa, No.8 Wilde Jagd |
| Voicing and lyricism | No.9 Ricordanza, No.11 Harmonies du soir |
| Stamina and control | No.10 Allegro agitato, No.12 Chasse-neige |

### Major Liszt targets
- Sonata in B minor
- Dante Sonata
- Mephisto Waltz No.1
- Hungarian Rhapsodies selected
- Transcendental Etudes selected or complete
- Paganini Etudes selected
- Années de pèlerinage cycles
- Paraphrases selected

### Beyond Liszt
- Alkan
- Godowsky
- Busoni
- Sorabji selected
- Ravel Gaspard
- Prokofiev Sonata No.8
- Bartók Sonata
- Ligeti Etudes
- Kapustin
- contemporary virtuoso works only if easy to source and genuinely useful

## Teacher and Feedback Checkpoints

| Stage | Suggested feedback frequency |
|---|---|
| Stage 0 to 2 | every 2 to 4 weeks |
| Stage 3 to 5 | every 4 to 6 weeks |
| Stage 6 to 7 | every 4 to 8 weeks |
| Stage 8 and beyond | every 2 to 6 weeks, possibly multiple coaches |

Ask specifically about:

- physical coordination
- tone production
- pedaling
- voicing
- style
- repertoire difficulty
- practice priorities

## Exam calibration only

- RCM and ABRSM can help estimate level.
- Do not let exam lists dictate musical value.
- Many obscure exam pieces are weaker than standard public-domain pieces that teach the same skill.
- If a piece is hard to find in a good edition, replace it.
- Use [Editions and Sources](Editions%20and%20Sources.md) to choose scores.

## Core Loop

Plan a specific result → attempt it → diagnose the limiting problem → choose the appropriate strategy → retest after a delay → perform under realistic conditions → maintain only what matters.
